research workspace

context

research gets scattered before it gets useful.

the more material a project gathers, the harder it becomes to keep track of what it says, where it came from and why it mattered in the first place.

we looked at how design students and UX researchers move between articles, interview notes, screenshots, shared boards and AI conversations. each tool handles one part of the process, but the researcher has to hold the whole thing together.

we kept coming back to a gap between tools that store information and tools that interpret it. a whiteboard gives you room to collect, but leaves the synthesis to you. a chatbot can move quickly, but can also move past the evidence and make decisions feel more settled than they are.

the passive end

everything goes in.

miro, figjam and similar canvases hold raw material well. they don't necessarily help you work out what connects or what deserves another look.

— the gap —
the active end

the answer comes out.

general LLMs can suggest directions, but references and context get buried in long chats. confident-sounding output still needs checking.

the first version tried to do everything.

we began by mapping the full ecosystem of things a researcher might need. it was useful as a landscape of possibilities, but it was too much product to make sense of at once.

the first version had ten features competing for attention. managing them simultaneously created task friction and fragmented the experience. we narrowed the workflow to compile, suggest and connect: keep the material together, help the researcher explore it, then make it easier to reach the right people or resources.

user testing · v2

three testers showed us where the notes-first flow broke down.

we watched how people tried to separate projects, get started and make sense of their notes. each point of friction led to a clear change in v3.

01 / KEEP PROJECTS APARTv2 → v3
“everything was in one big pile of notes.”
what we learned

research from different projects needed a clear boundary.

what changed

each project now has its own overview and research brief.

02 / KNOW HOW TO BEGINv2 → v3
“I stared at the canvas. I did not know what to type.”
what we learned

a blank canvas gave people no clear first step.

what changed

chat became the starting point; useful passages can be saved as notes.

03 / TRUST AND RETRIEVE NOTESv2 → v3
“I have a note that people trust a person over an app. Did I hear that, or assume it?”
what we learned

people needed to know what a note represented and find it again.

what changed

notes became typed and filterable, with search across the workspace.

the remove-it test

01 / project context

give each project its own context.

projects keep research separate, with a brief for the question, scope and audience.

02 / chat

start with a conversation.

chat gives the researcher a familiar place to think out loud while the project keeps its context close.

03 / notes

turn useful moments into notes.

typed notes help distinguish kinds of evidence, then filter it down when the researcher needs it again.

04 / workspace

keep the research connected in one workspace.

move across projects, chats, notes and people without losing the context that connects them.

limitations

the interface is further along than the agent.

the prototype demonstrates the structure and core interactions, but it doesn't yet validate the full product idea.

Project setup, chats, saving notes, note filters, workspace search and the people directory are represented in the current preview. Agent replies are scripted, automated synthesis is not implemented, and the Themes page is under construction. We collected qualitative feedback from three tests, not quantitative usability results. The next step is to test the core tasks with first-time users and build the agent capabilities against reliable sources.

looking back

making the product smaller made the workflow clearer.

the most useful changes came from noticing small moments of friction: not knowing where a note belonged, not knowing how to begin, or not being able to tell an observation from an assumption.

the agent should help someone work through their research, not decide what the research means. keeping that distinction clear shaped what we kept, what we changed and what we left for later.
