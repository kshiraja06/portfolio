context

design students drown in their own research.

as design students ourselves, we initially approached this problem by mapping every feature, touchpoint, and capability we wished we had during our own studio research workflows. i ideated and finalized the features and the idea, then built the final prototype myself.

aishaa visual communication / HCD student balancing chaotic studio research: “i don't want AI to write my project for me, i just need a tool that turns my research clutter into clear connections and lets me pick where to go next.”

rohanan independent junior UX researcher without a senior lead steering the workflow.

mayaa design student who needs specialized human mentorship once online research stops helping.

the problem

existing tools are either passive or decisive.

passive canvases: miro, figjam, and canva whiteboards hold raw research but do not help users synthesize it.

decisive chatbots: chatgpt, claude, and perplexity can leapfrog the creative process by telling users what to think, and can generate plausible-sounding but unverified precedents or fake references.

the solution

waypoint is a quiet workspace that files your notes, connects you to people, and checks in on the brief. the final preview includes project setup, chats, typed notes, search, and a shared people list with a faculty directory.

ten features became three

v1it initially had ten features. managing them simultaneously created too much task friction and fragmented the user experience.

v2we consolidated into a three-stage pipeline: compile, suggest, connect. four main features: notes, themes, discover, people.

v3three musts. the information architecture changed with it.

three user tests

tested on v2, reflected in v3.

two projects, one pileresearch in one flat workspace could not be separated by project. projects became the container, each with an overview and an editable research brief.

blank canvas, no starting pointa notes-first canvas gave no entry point. v3 starts from a familiar chat and sidebar layout, and passages are saved from chats into notes.

evidence i could not tell apart“i have a note that people trust a person over an app. did i hear that, or assume it?” notes are typed as observation, assumption, question, care, or resource, and are filterable. search spans the workspace.

what we cut

the remove-it test: would the product still make sense without it?

chat and notes

no place for evidence. product loses its purpose.

people

no human bridge. the differentiator is gone.

context check-in

the agent drifts from the user's brief.

themes

manual sorting, workflow still usable. deferred to the next build.

what's still missing

qualitative comparison only. no quantitative results were collected. demo replies are scripted, and the themes page is under construction. validation remains proposed.

takeaways

  • the agent helps and never decides. strategic control stays with the user.
  • user tests reshaped an agent. i build, watch, then cut.
