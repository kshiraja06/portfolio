community kitchen · interactive experience

we are in a soup

a student-run community kitchen reminding you to slow down.

the project

we are in a soup started with the idea of creating a space where people could slow down and connect with each other. in a world where we're constantly exposed to bad news and everything happening around us, we wanted to bring the focus back to the people immediately around us.

we ran a community kitchen where people could spend time together, make food and talk about issues happening around the world. the website is a digital recreation of that space, allowing people to move through it and take part in different activities.

the idea

we wanted the website to feel like a place people could enter and explore, rather than a page that simply tells them what the project is about.

the experience is built around four tables. visitors can paint a bowl, cook a soup, leave a worry, and learn how to build a community kitchen of their own. each table gives them something to do, and they can move between them at their own pace.

the idea was to make the website an extension of the physical space, not just a documentation of it.

the four tables

01. paint a bowlvisitors can paint and customise a bowl using the digital painting interface.

02. cook a soupthe cooking station recreates the process of preparing a simple soup, giving visitors a moment to pause and take part in the kitchen.

03. leave a worryvisitors can write down something that's weighing on them and submit it. they can also view worries left by other people.

04. build a community kitchenthe final table leads to information on how to build a community kitchen, extending the project beyond the website itself.

once all four tables have been visited, a cutscene presents statistics about what's happening around the world. it ends with the message: feed a friend / help a friend.

recreating the space

the website recreates the community kitchen as an interactive environment. visitors move around using the WASD keys, and certain objects light up to indicate that they can be interacted with.

we wanted people to discover the different activities by moving through the space themselves. instead of presenting everything at once, the website gives them a reason to explore.

the different stations also have their own interactions. the bowl painting and glazing studios let visitors work directly on a bowl, while the cooking station takes them through the preparation of a soup.

design decisions

why recreate the physical space?we wanted the website to feel connected to the community kitchen we had actually run. recreating the space gave visitors a way to experience the project online, rather than just reading about it.

what happens after showing people the news?the cutscene presents statistics about what's happening around the world, but we didn't want the experience to end with just that information. it ends with an action: feed a friend / help a friend.

how does the project continue beyond the website?the fourth table introduces visitors to the process of building a community kitchen. the intention was to make the project something people could take forward themselves.

why is there no winner?the experience isn't built around winning or completing a challenge. the activities are there to give people a reason to spend time in the space. the point is the interaction itself.

what went wrong

the website is technically quite messy, and its structure could have been better organised. the UX also needs a lot of improvement. visitors are given the freedom to move around, but there aren't always enough guidelines to help them understand what they can interact with or where they should go.

the website is currently under maintenance. looking back, clearer instructions and a more considered structure would have made the experience easier to navigate without taking away from the freedom to explore.

takeaways

the project made us think about how a digital experience can carry the feeling of a physical space. giving visitors control over where they go and what they interact with changes the way they experience the project.

at the same time, freedom to explore doesn't mean leaving people completely on their own. there still need to be enough cues and guidelines for them to understand the space.

the main thing we wanted to create was a reason for people to spend time together. the website was one way of extending that idea.

credits: we are in a soup was a collaborative project. thank you to avnit for facilitating, everyone involved in the project, and the staff who helped us run the kitchen.
